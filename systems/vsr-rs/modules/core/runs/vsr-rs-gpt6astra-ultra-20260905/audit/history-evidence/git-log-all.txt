    Lean: the quorum-intersection lemma
    
    Vsr/Quorum.lean proves that in a cluster of n replicas, where a quorum is
    n/2+1, any two quorums of distinct replica ids share a member: a Finset
    pigeonhole from Mathlib. Stated both directly and over Config.
    
    This is the keystone of the safety endgame. onPrepareOk (a new
    acknowledgement completing a quorum), the view-change handlers (the chosen
    log carries every committed entry), and durability all rest on it.
    
    The library now depends on Mathlib for it, adding the Classical.choice
    axiom. Main.lean, the vsr-replay executable, imports only Vsr.Check rather
    than the umbrella so it stays free of Mathlib.

    Keep the view-change backoff until the new view has proved stable
    
    A view change could start the next one, forever. With a primary timeout of
    two idle periods, a heartbeat every tick, and no delay, a view change
    takes exactly as long as a replica is willing to wait for it, and the
    simulator runs idle periods before deliveries within a tick, so a replica
    that joined the change with the shortest wait timed out one tick before
    StartView reached it and started another view. The backoff exists for
    this, doubling the wait for every view change entered from another, but
    enter_normal reset it to zero. The replica that had just completed a view
    was therefore always the one with the shortest wait in the next view
    change, and always the one to cut it short, round the ring. On a perfect
    network the view number rose by one every two ticks for the whole
    million-tick convergence budget.
    
    The backoff now survives enter_normal and is forgotten only after
    Config::primary_timeout consecutive idle periods of stable normal
    operation, counted in a new field, idle_periods_stable. A replica pulled
    into a view change right after completing one keeps its longer wait, so
    each replica needs at most one cut-short view change to wait long enough,
    and the storm ends. Once the cluster is calm for a full timeout the
    counter returns to zero as before.
    
    Found by scripts/simulate at commit 19d71e51f41758622b7b00285e6f6634f33e2f8b:
    
        vsr-simulator --lite 7098080745341283894
        vsr-simulator --lite 6658838664322981494
    
    Both failed with "no state convergence: pending message" at ticks 1003341
    and 1021549; both pass now. The regression test is
    test_view_change_does_not_start_the_next in tests/cluster.rs, on a new
    step method of the hand-driven cluster that delivers with a one-tick
    latency in the simulator's order; it climbed to view 251 in 500 steps
    before the fix.
    
    The Lean model caught it too, once it could say anything about progress.
    Vsr/Liveness.lean adds a synchronous scheduler and the liveness property
    settles, from any reachable state with a quorum not recovering. On the
    model before the fix, theorems proved by running the model in the kernel
    refuted it for the regression scenario: no settled round among 500, view
    251 after 500 rounds, and a run periodic in everything but the view
    number. The same theorems now prove the scenario settles within eight
    rounds in view 3. Check.liveness runs the bounded property after every
    step of a conformance trace; it fired on 27 of the 40 traces before the
    fix and on none now. The twin mirrors the fix, and the conformance test
    passes.

    Lean: prove the first two layers of the safety invariant
    
    The safety theorem needs an inductive invariant over the whole cluster.
    This proves its first two layers and leaves the rest as sorry.
    
    Layer one is the per-replica invariant in Vsr/Local.lean: the commit
    number is within the log, the last normal view is not ahead of the view
    and equals it in normal status and state transfer, a replica catching up
    is in view-change status, and a recovering replica holds nothing. Every
    handler, idle period, and recovery preserves it with no assumption about
    the messages received. Lifted to the system it proves
    commitBounded_of_reachable, the first of the four properties.
    
    Layer two is well-formedness of every message ever sent, in
    Vsr/WellFormed.lean: log lengths match the op numbers carried, commit
    numbers do not exceed them, a vote's last normal view is not ahead of its
    view, and a recovery state's commit number is within its log. Every
    handler only sends well-formed messages because its sender satisfies
    layer one. Lifted to the system it proves sentWF_of_reachable.
    
    Vsr/Frame.lean holds the frame lemmas both run on: what each helper of
    the replica leaves alone, as simp lemmas. Two handlers, onRequest and
    onStartViewChange, lost the Option tricks in their definitions so the
    proofs split cleanly; the conformance test confirmed the rewrites change
    nothing.
    
    Vsr/Check.lean has a Bool version of every invariant, proved or
    candidate, and vsr-replay evaluates them after every step of a trace,
    reporting violations on stderr; the conformance test fails on any. The
    candidates for the remaining layers, one log per view, committed means
    quorum-acknowledged, and committed entries survive later views, held on
    240 seeds. Those three form one combined invariant, since a replica
    catching up keeps its committed prefix and takes the rest from the new
    view, which only agrees because committed entries survive. Mathlib is in
    the lakefile for the quorum intersection lemma that needs; nothing
    imports it yet.
    
    Durability was restated to match the simulator: it counts holders among
    replicas that are not recovering, since a replica back from a crash holds
    nothing until it has recovered. The version that counted every replica
    was false.
    
    lean/README.md lists the file layout, what is proved and what is not, the
    plan for the rest, how the model is checked against the code, and the
    proof conventions that got the handler lemmas through.

    Replace the Kani harnesses with a Lean model and a conformance check
    
    The Kani harnesses never finished. The replica keeps five B-trees, and
    CBMC unrolls the standard library's node code for every map operation
    and even for Drop, so the fully concrete two-step harness ran twenty
    minutes and six gigabytes without producing a formula. The symbolic
    harnesses branch on top of that, and their unwind bounds sit below the
    B-tree node capacity, so they would have failed on unwinding assertions
    regardless. Bounded model checking of a whole cluster is the wrong shape
    of problem for Kani; the simulator already explores interleavings faster
    than CBMC can encode one.
    
    lean/ holds a model of the replica in Lean 4: one definition per Rust
    function with the same control flow, B-trees as sorted association lists
    so the tie-breaking in the view-change vote matches, and every assert!
    as a panicked flag so the handlers are total. The cluster model keeps
    every message ever sent, so delivery covers loss, delay, replay, and
    reordering with one rule. Safety is stated over reachable states: no
    panic, commit bounded by log length, committed-prefix agreement, and
    durability in a quorum. The main theorem is a sorry; what is proved is
    that commit numbers never move backwards.
    
    verify/ ties the model to the code. It generates seeded traces of cluster
    steps, runs the real replicas and the model through each, and diffs what
    they print after every step. Forty seeds of two hundred steps pass, on
    three and five replicas, reaching view 5, state transfer, and recovery.
    Making the model skip commits on Commit messages is caught at step 29 of
    seed 0. The test passes with a note when no Lean toolchain is installed.

    question worth answering is which lines it reaches, not which lines the
    unit tests reach.
    
    scripts/coverage builds the simulator with LLVM source-based
    instrumentation, runs a batch of random seeds on every core the way
    scripts/simulate does, and merges the profiles into one report: a per-file
    summary on the terminal, annotated sources in target/coverage/report.txt,
    and HTML in target/coverage/html.

    invalidate the normal build cache, and a failing seed does not stop the
    batch: the report is still written and the command to reproduce the
    failure is printed.

    Deterministic simulator
    
    A simulator that drives a cluster of replicas and clients over a
    simulated network, seeded so every run is reproducible from its seed. It
    injects faults, loss, replay, reordering delay, crashes, restarts,
    reboots through recovery, and partitions, and checks the safety

    random seeds for a time budget and reports per commit. A terminal viewer
    draws the cluster as it runs. Every bug in this repository was found by
    it.

    Initial commit
    
    A work-in-progress implementation of Viewstamped Replication, following
    "Viewstamped Replication Revisited" by Liskov and Cowling. The library
    does no I/O, keeps no clocks, and starts no threads: a Replica and a
    Client are state machines their owner steps with on_message, on_idle, and
    on_reply, and drains what they want sent. Normal operation, state
    transfer, view changes, and recovery are all here. tests/cluster.rs
    drives a small cluster by hand.
