`FailureAtomicity`, `ExactlyOnceCompletion`, `ObjectIdentityStability`, and
`ResourceAccountingBalance` correspond to FAILURE-ATOMICITY,
EXACTLY-ONCE-COMPLETION, OBJECT-IDENTITY-STABILITY, and
RESOURCE-ACCOUNTING-BALANCE. EAGAIN cannot publish a registration. A timeout
removes its own registration and never undoes previous migration. Every queue
or extracted owner accounts for exactly one registration token. Closure is
separate from notification, so two accepted timer/futex notifications before
close do not falsely violate exactly-once completion.

The resource property applies after bucket operations, cleanup and outstanding
callback strong references drain. It excludes weak canceled timer heap nodes.
Its source-backed obligation comes from `futex.rs:121-124`, global bucket
ownership at `384-475`, and the absence of a deferred FutexItem reaper in the
modeled operations. A failure is a reclamation candidate requiring downstream
review. It is not, by itself, a Linux-visible incompatibility claim.

The live-migration property snapshots exactly two live source registrations
with no pending wake or completion immediately before the locked wake scan.
It checks distinct keys and raw (1,1). Per-waiter noise epochs and the current deadline check disqualify
independent expiry, timer notification or closure during the operation.
Admission and other bucket operations cannot interleave because both locks
remain held. The eligible cohort minus the accepted wakes must be included
in the actual moved set. The model makes no FIFO ABI promise.

`PermittedWaitResult` permits spurious success, checks no early timeout, and
rejects the otherwise unreachable restart result in this signal-free scope.
`RequeueQuotaAndResult` is split into `RequeueWakeQuota`, `RequeueMoveQuota`,
and `RequeueReturnCount` for independent controls. Its same-address processed
count is an observer for the pinned Linux behavior, not an implementation move.
Compatibility claims still require a Linux control with its actual kernel ID.

The standard finite universe has A and C in bucket 1 and B in bucket 2,
four unique waiter tokens, four call tokens, two reusable controller actors,
word values 0/1, and quotas 0/1/2. Zero denotes an absent numeric ID. Uniform
owner tuples distinguish none, local, bucket and transfer ownership. Durations
use `Never`, `After(0)` and `After(1)` records with boolean/integer fields. The MC budget record bounds admissions,
timed admissions, user stores and recoverable faults. It never bounds normal
reactive steps. Clock advancement stops only when there is no pending future
deadline, including a saved deadline awaiting publication. Message-buffer limits do not apply. `FiniteCapacity` is a consequence
of unique registration tokens and does not prune valid queue occupancy.
Only controller actors are symmetric. Keys, bucket indices and generation
ordinals have fixed workload roles. Budgets remain in the state view.

`MC_hunt_completion.cfg` uses `MCFairSpec` and enables
`ConditionalCompletion`, `TimedCompletion` and `ControlCompletion` without
symmetry reduction. Fairness applies to each enabled waiter, timer callback,
control operation, collection boundary, and clock. Finite interference/faults are explicit. A WAIT
without an accepted notification or timeout has no completion obligation.
No watchdog timeout is treated as a kernel progress failure.

## Review corrections and validation boundary

The round-2 findings are the only input to the current revision. P2-heap adds
`HeapHeads` to both pop and collection-end decisions (`timer.rs:207-215,299-304`).
Cancelled nodes retain their original deadline, so cancellation does not permit
out-of-order removal. P2-callbacks retains each invocation's vector independently
in `s.callbacks`. Only its head may start, and the next callback waits for return,
including weak-upgrade failure (`timer.rs:220-221,262-281`). Other invocations
remain able to overlap after heap release. Trace v3 binds every callback phase
to the originating `(pid,batch)` until the vector drains and checks its pending
suffix. This refines the multi-timer histories in scenario 3 without changing
the ownership, quota or live-migration oracles.

The following round-1 corrections remain intact.

P1 replaces all mixed sentinel/payload comparisons and updates v2 NDJSON
normalization and the post-state schema. P2a preserves generation-local reads
in `TimerSetClock` and `PauseTimeoutClock`, and the one batch sample from
`TimerCollectClock` through `TimerCollectEnd`. `ClockAdvance` changes neither
sample. Deadline publication uses `timer.rs:67-68`, remaining time uses
`106-109`, and collection uses `206-215`. The additional read PCs remain
eligible for the same workloads and observers as their unsplit predecessor.

P2b adds `WaiterSpuriousReturn`, following the scheduler contract at
`ostd/src/task/scheduler/mod.rs:441-444` and the retry loop at
`ostd/src/sync/wait.rs:272-275`. It leaves wake acceptance and completion
observers unchanged. All 57 primitive actions have one direct trace wrapper
and instrumentation row. Every wrapper retains mandatory post-state equality,
and there are no silent kernel actions.

All 18 original configurations retain their bounds and checks. The audit
script verifies this against the workspace's original Git content. Each TLC
run uses at most 2 GiB, two workers, one process at a time and 1,200 seconds.
Metadata is stored under `../states/` and every log under `generation-checks/`.
The synthetic fixtures are generated histories, not kernel captures.
Instrumentation, implementation traces, guest execution, Linux controls,
confirmation and classification remain `NOT_RUN`.
